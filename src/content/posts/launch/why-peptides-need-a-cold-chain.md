## Peptides are chains, and chains can break

A peptide is a sequence of amino acids linked together. That structure is what makes it work, and it's also what makes it fragile. Heat provides energy that speeds up chemical reactions, including the ones that break those links apart, a process generally called degradation.

### Why small molecules don't have this problem

A typical tablet contains a small, simple molecule that's chemically stable at room temperature for years. A peptide's larger, more complex structure has far more ways to break down, which is why the storage requirements for the two look so different.

## What "cold chain" actually means

A cold chain is an unbroken sequence of temperature-controlled handling from the point of manufacture through to the point of use: controlled storage at the manufacturer, insulated and often ice-packed shipping, and controlled storage again once it arrives. Breaking that chain at any point, even temporarily, has a cumulative effect.

### Why "unbroken" is the key word

A cold chain that's mostly maintained but breaks for one leg of the journey doesn't get full credit for the legs that were done correctly. The whole point is continuity, since even a partial lapse contributes to degradation that can't be undone by resuming cold storage afterward.

## Why this matters for buyers

Understanding the cold chain concept is what makes questions like "does this seller ship with ice packs" and "how long was this in transit" meaningful, rather than arbitrary details to check off a list.
