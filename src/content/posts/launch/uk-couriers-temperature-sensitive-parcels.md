## Standard courier handling, not specialist cold-chain logistics

Most UK peptide suppliers use standard parcel courier networks (Royal Mail, or major private couriers) rather than specialist medical cold-chain logistics firms. That means the insulation and ice packs inside the box are doing all the temperature-control work; the courier itself typically isn't providing refrigerated handling.

### Where this creates risk

A parcel sitting in a hot delivery van, in a sorting depot overnight, or left on a doorstep in direct sun are all points where a standard courier's handling doesn't account for what's inside the box. This is precisely why the insulation and ice pack inside matter so much.

## What "next-day delivery" actually covers

Next-day delivery guarantees relate to timing, not temperature. A courier meeting its next-day commitment says nothing about whether the parcel was kept cool throughout that day, since standard courier services don't monitor or guarantee internal parcel temperature.

### Remote and rural routes

Areas with longer standard delivery windows, including parts of Scotland, Northern Ireland and some island postcodes, extend the total time a parcel is in the courier network, which increases the importance of robust packaging for those specific routes.

## What buyers can realistically control

Buyers can't change how a courier handles a parcel in transit, but they can choose suppliers who account for it with proper insulation, and they can choose delivery to an address where the parcel won't sit outside for hours after arrival.
