## Yes, gradually, and unevenly

Every peptide loses some potency over time; the question is how fast. Retatrutide is no exception. The rate depends heavily on storage conditions: a vial kept refrigerated, in the dark, and used within its recommended window loses very little. A vial left warm, exposed to light, or used well past a recommended window can lose meaningfully more.

### Why potency loss isn't usually visible

Unlike spoiled food, a peptide that's lost some potency often looks identical to a fresh one. There's no reliable way to tell from appearance alone whether a vial is still at full strength, which is exactly why following storage guidance matters even when nothing looks obviously wrong.

## What actually degrades the peptide

Heat accelerates a chemical breakdown of the peptide's amino acid chain. Light exposure adds oxidative stress. Repeated temperature swings, like a vial going in and out of the fridge frequently, compound both effects over time.

### Does this happen at a predictable rate

Degradation isn't perfectly linear, and it varies by specific peptide, formulation, and how it's stored. This is why storage guidance tends to give a recommended window rather than promising an exact expiry point.

## Reducing potency loss

Consistent refrigeration, minimal light exposure, using reconstituted vials within the recommended window, and avoiding unnecessary handling all reduce the rate of potency loss over a vial's usable life.
