## Reconstitution starts a shorter clock

Adding bacteriostatic water to a lyophilised peptide moves it from a relatively stable dry state into a far more fragile liquid one. From that point, refrigeration and time both matter in a way they didn't before.

### The standard advice

Refrigerated storage at 2-8°C, away from light, is the common recommendation across most injectable peptides once reconstituted. Most guidance points to a usable window of roughly 2-4 weeks, though this varies by specific peptide and isn't a universal figure.

## Why bacteriostatic water matters here

Bacteriostatic water contains a small amount of benzyl alcohol, which helps limit bacterial growth in the reconstituted solution. It doesn't stop chemical degradation of the peptide itself, which is why refrigeration and a limited usage window still apply even with bacteriostatic water rather than plain sterile water.

### Handling between doses

Drawing up a dose introduces a small risk of contamination each time a vial is accessed. Cleaning the vial top with an alcohol swab before each draw, and returning the vial to the fridge promptly, are both standard practice for minimising that risk over a vial's usable life.

## When to stop using a vial

Visible cloudiness, particles, or a colour change from what the solution looked like when freshly mixed are reasons to stop using a vial regardless of how many weeks have passed. When in doubt, discard rather than use.
