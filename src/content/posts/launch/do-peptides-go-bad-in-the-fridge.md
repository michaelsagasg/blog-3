## Refrigeration slows degradation, it doesn't stop it

Even under ideal refrigerated conditions, a peptide's potency declines gradually over time. Refrigeration is about slowing that process down to a manageable rate, not pausing it indefinitely. This is why storage guidance still gives a recommended usable window even for a properly refrigerated vial.

### Why an expiry-style window still applies

A reconstituted peptide kept perfectly refrigerated still has a finite recommended window, commonly a few weeks, because the presence of water accelerates degradation regardless of temperature. Cold slows the clock; it doesn't stop it.

## What else affects the outcome, besides temperature

How often the fridge door is opened near where the vial sits, whether the vial is exposed to light, and how many times it's handled to draw a dose all contribute, on top of the baseline temperature.

### The fridge door isn't the best spot

Door shelves see more temperature fluctuation than the main body of a fridge every time the door opens. Storing peptide vials further back, away from the door, gives more consistent temperature exposure.

## The bottom line

A fridge is necessary, not sufficient, for keeping a peptide good. Combined with protection from light, minimal handling, and respecting the recommended usable window, it does its job well; on its own, it only slows the clock.
