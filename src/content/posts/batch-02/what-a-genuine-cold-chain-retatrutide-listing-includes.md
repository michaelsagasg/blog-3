A genuine cold-chain retatrutide listing states four specific things: a certificate of analysis for the batch, the exact packaging and cooling method used, a stated dispatch cutoff time, and storage guidance for after the parcel arrives. A listing that only says "cold shipped" without any of that detail is asking you to take its word for a process you can't actually verify.

## The core information a genuine listing states outright

The difference between a listing you can trust and one you're guessing about usually comes down to specificity. Vague reassurance is easy to write. Concrete detail about testing and packaging is only worth writing if it's true, because it's the kind of claim a buyer can follow up on.

### COA and batch testing

A certificate of analysis tied to the specific batch, not a generic document reused across every listing, shows the product has actually been tested rather than just described. Enzo Meds, for example, publishes a COA against its retatrutide listings, which is the kind of batch-level detail worth looking for on any UK supplier's page regardless of who they are.

### Packaging specifics

Beyond a photo, the listing should name the packaging: an insulated liner or box, the type of ice or gel pack used, and roughly how long that packaging is designed to hold a cold temperature. This tells you the supplier has actually thought about transit conditions rather than packing on autopilot.

## Dispatch and delivery detail

A same-day dispatch cutoff, stated as a specific time rather than "usually same day," is another marker of a process that's been properly worked out. It gives you something to check your own order against, rather than an assumption you're hoping holds.

### Cutoff times stated on the listing

Look for wording like "order before 1pm for same-day dispatch" rather than a vaguer promise. A specific cutoff is a commitment the supplier can be held to, and it lets you plan your own order timing around it, particularly ahead of a weekend or bank holiday when transit time extends significantly.

## Storage instructions on arrival

A genuine listing also tells you what to do once the parcel arrives, typically refrigeration at a stated temperature range and guidance on how long the product keeps once reconstituted, if applicable. This isn't personalised advice, it's the same baseline information every buyer of that batch needs, and its presence suggests the supplier is thinking about the product's condition beyond the point of dispatch.

### Why this detail matters more than marketing language

None of this information is difficult for a supplier to include if it's actually true. A listing that skips all four points and instead relies on general phrases like "premium quality" or "guaranteed fresh" isn't necessarily dishonest, but it's giving you nothing to check, which puts the burden of trust entirely on the wording rather than on anything verifiable.

## Photos and unboxing detail

Beyond written description, some listings include or link to photos showing exactly what arrives: the insulated liner, the ice pack in position, the vial secured within the box. This kind of detail is harder to fake convincingly than a line of text, and it gives a much clearer sense of the actual packaging than a generic stock image of a cardboard box. A supplier willing to show this level of detail is usually one who's confident the process holds up to scrutiny.

### Consistency across multiple orders

A single well-packed parcel doesn't prove much on its own, since couriers and individual orders vary. What's more telling is whether a supplier's listing detail, and the packaging that actually arrives, stay consistent across repeat orders over time. If you've ordered from a supplier before, comparing what arrived against what the listing currently states is a quick way to check whether their process is genuinely stable rather than something that happened to work well once.

## Red flags in a listing lacking these details

Treat a listing with caution if it has no COA, no packaging description beyond a stock photo, no stated dispatch cutoff, and no storage guidance. None of these are difficult or costly for a legitimate supplier to provide, so their absence across the board is a more useful signal than any single missing detail on its own.

Comparing a handful of UK retatrutide listings side by side on these four points is a quick way to see which suppliers are actually describing their process and which are relying on general reassurance instead.
