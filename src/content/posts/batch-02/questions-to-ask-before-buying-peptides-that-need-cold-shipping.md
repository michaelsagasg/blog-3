## Ask about packaging before you ask about price

The single most useful question to ask before ordering is what the parcel is actually packed with: insulation type, ice pack details, and whether that changes based on distance or season. A specific answer tells you more about reliability than a low price ever will.

### "What's your same-day dispatch cutoff?"

A stated cutoff time, commonly somewhere between 11am and 2pm, tells you whether your order goes out today or tomorrow. This affects total transit time directly, which matters more for a temperature-sensitive item than an ordinary parcel. If a seller can't give you a clear cutoff time, that's worth noting.

## Questions about the packaging itself

- What insulation material is used: foam box, foil liner, thermal bag?
- What type of ice pack, and how many per parcel?
- Does packaging change for longer UK routes, such as the Scottish Highlands, islands, or Northern Ireland?
- Is packaging adjusted seasonally, with more ice packs used in summer?

### Why these matter together, not individually

Any single answer here could sound fine in isolation, but a supplier who can answer all of them specifically and consistently is demonstrating an actual documented process. One who answers only the first question and deflects on the rest is more likely improvising per order.

## Ask what happens if something goes wrong

"What do you do if a delivery is delayed and the parcel arrives warm?" is worth asking directly, ideally before you order rather than after a problem occurs. A supplier with a clear answer, whether that's a reship policy or a replacement process, is signalling they've thought about failure cases, not just the ideal scenario.

### Ask about documentation

"Can I see a certificate of analysis (COA) for this batch?" is a reasonable and increasingly standard question. A COA confirms the product has been tested for identity and purity, and a supplier who provides one readily, matched to the specific batch rather than a generic document reused across orders, is generally more transparent across the board.

### Ask how long they've been shipping this way

"How long have you been dispatching with this packaging setup?" can be a useful follow-up, since a supplier who's refined their process over many orders tends to give a more confident, specific answer than one describing something they only recently put together. Neither answer is disqualifying on its own, but it adds useful context to everything else you've asked.

## Questions about delivery logistics

- Which courier do you use, and what's the typical transit time to my postcode?
- Is a signature required, or can it be left in a safe place?
- Can I choose a specific delivery day, or select a collection point?

### Why delivery flexibility matters for temperature-sensitive parcels

A parcel that can only be left on a doorstep if nobody's home spends extra hours exposed to ambient temperature, undoing some of the benefit of good packaging. A supplier or courier that offers a collection point option, or lets you specify a safe place, gives you more control over the final stretch of the journey.

## Questions worth asking yourself, not just the seller

- Will I actually be home, or nearby, on the expected delivery day?
- Do I have fridge space ready before the parcel arrives?
- Am I ordering with enough buffer before a weekend or bank holiday, when transit times often extend?

### Timing your order around your own schedule

Even the best-packed parcel benefits from being planned around a day you're actually available. Ordering on a Monday or Tuesday for weekday delivery, rather than late in the week, reduces the chance of a delay pushing receipt into a weekend when you might not be checking for it as closely.

## Putting the answers together

No single question here is a dealbreaker on its own, but a pattern of vague or evasive answers across several of them is a reasonable reason to look elsewhere. A supplier who answers specifically, provides documentation readily, and has a clear plan for delays is showing you an actual process, which is what you're really trying to verify before you commit to an order.

## Bottom line

Before buying peptides that need cold shipping, ask about packaging specifics, dispatch timing, failure handling, and available documentation. Combine that with a realistic look at your own availability to receive the parcel, and you've covered the two halves of the cold chain that are actually within anyone's control.
