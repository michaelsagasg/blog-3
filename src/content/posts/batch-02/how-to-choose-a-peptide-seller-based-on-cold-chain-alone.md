Judge a peptide seller on cold chain alone by checking four things in this order: how specifically they describe their packaging, what their dispatch cutoff actually guarantees, whether they offer any evidence beyond a claim, and what they promise if something goes wrong. Price and brand reputation matter for other reasons, but none of them tell you whether a parcel will actually arrive at a safe temperature, which is what this framework is for.

## Ignore price for a moment

A cheaper listing with no packaging detail at all is a false saving if the product arrives having spent hours outside a safe temperature range. Before comparing prices between sellers, set price aside and run each one through the same four checks below. Only compare cost between sellers who pass all four reasonably well, since comparing price across sellers with meaningfully different cold-chain processes isn't really comparing like with like.

## The packaging description test

Read the shipping or packaging section of the listing, not the marketing copy on the product page itself. A seller who states the insulation type, the ice pack type, and whether either changes for distance or season is describing an actual process. A seller who only writes "shipped with care" or "cold chain maintained" is describing an intention, which isn't the same thing.

### What to do if the listing is vague

Message the seller directly and ask the same three questions the listing should have answered: what's the insulation, what's the ice pack, and does packaging change for longer routes. A specific, confident answer is a reasonable substitute for a detailed listing. Hesitation or a generic reply is itself useful information.

## The dispatch and timing test

A stated same-day dispatch cutoff tells you how much of the transit window is actually spent moving rather than waiting to be picked up. Check that cutoff against a realistic UK delivery time for your postcode, particularly if you're in a part of Scotland, Northern Ireland, or an island postcode where standard transit times run longer than the UK average. A seller who dispatches next-day courier as standard, with a clearly stated cutoff, is giving the ice pack the best possible chance of still being effective on arrival.

### Weekday-only dispatch matters here too

If a seller only dispatches on weekdays, check what happens to an order placed on a Thursday or Friday. A parcel that sits over a weekend before dispatch, or is dispatched Friday and sits with a courier over the weekend, faces a longer effective transit time than the advertised delivery speed suggests.

## The evidence test

Beyond a written description, look for anything that provides evidence rather than just a claim: a temperature indicator packed inside the box that shows whether a safe threshold was exceeded, and a certificate of analysis (COA) for the specific batch confirming what's actually in the vial. Neither is universal across UK sellers, but their presence signals a seller willing to be checked rather than simply trusted. A seller who publishes a COA against every listing as standard, rather than only on request, is giving you a benchmark worth using when comparing across sellers.

### Reviews that mention handling specifically

Star ratings alone don't tell you much about cold-chain performance. Reviews describing the parcel's actual condition on arrival, insulation intact, ice pack still cold, delivered on time, are far more useful, and a pattern of complaints about warm parcels across multiple reviews is worth weighing heavily even against an otherwise strong rating.

## The guarantee test

Ask, or check the returns policy for, what happens if a parcel arrives warm. A seller with a genuine process states a clear remedy, replacement, refund, or reship, and what evidence they need from you, typically photos of the packaging and any temperature indicator. A seller with no stated policy, or one that only offers help after a public complaint, is a weaker bet regardless of how good their packaging looks on paper.

## Putting it together

A seller who scores well across all four checks, specific packaging description, sensible dispatch timing, some form of independent evidence, and a clear guarantee, is demonstrating an actual cold-chain process rather than a marketing phrase. Weigh price and everything else only after this filter, not before it.
