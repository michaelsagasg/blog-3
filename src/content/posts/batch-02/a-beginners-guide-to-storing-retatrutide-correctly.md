Refrigerate retatrutide at 2-8°C as soon as it arrives, keep it away from light, and never let it freeze. That's the short version. The longer version, covering why each of those rules exists and what changes once you reconstitute it, is worth knowing before your first order arrives rather than working it out afterwards.

## Before you open the box

Check the parcel's packaging first: whether the insulation and ice pack still show some residual cold tells you roughly how well the cold chain held during transit. Retatrutide typically ships as a lyophilised, freeze-dried powder, which is meaningfully more heat-tolerant in transit than a reconstituted liquid would be, but it still benefits from prompt refrigeration once it's in your hands rather than sitting on a counter for the rest of the day.

### Move it to the fridge promptly

As soon as reasonably possible after delivery, move the vial to the fridge. If you know you'll be out when a delivery is due, arrange a safe place with the courier or a neighbour who can take it in, since a well-packed parcel left on a doorstep in direct sun for several hours undoes a fair amount of the seller's cold-chain effort.

## Fridge storage basics

### The 2-8°C range, and why it's specific

Most guidance for retatrutide and similar peptides calls for refrigerated storage between 2 and 8°C. This range is cold enough to slow the chemical degradation reactions that break down peptide structure over time, without dropping low enough to risk freezing. A kitchen thermometer left in the fridge for a day is a cheap way to confirm your fridge actually sits in this range, since many home fridges run colder or warmer than their dial suggests.

### Avoid the door shelf

The door of a fridge experiences the widest temperature swings, since it's exposed to room temperature air every time the fridge is opened. Store the vial on a shelf inside the main body of the fridge instead, ideally somewhere it won't be knocked or need to be moved often.

## What happens if it freezes

Freezing isn't generally recommended for retatrutide, and this is worth understanding rather than just following as a rule. When a solution freezes, water molecules form ice crystals, and those crystals can physically disrupt the folded structure that gives a peptide its function. Even once thawed, a peptide that's been through this process may no longer be structurally intact in the way it was before freezing, and there's no visible way to confirm this by looking at the vial.

### Lyophilised powder is more forgiving

Before reconstitution, retatrutide exists as a freeze-dried powder rather than a liquid, and removing the water removes the medium most degradation reactions, including ice crystal damage, need to happen in. This is why unopened lyophilised vials tolerate brief temperature lapses during shipping far better than a mixed solution does, though refrigeration is still the standard recommendation for storage once it's in your possession.

## After reconstitution

Once bacteriostatic water is added, retatrutide becomes considerably more sensitive to both temperature and time. Continue refrigerated storage at 2-8°C, and most guidance for similar peptides points to using a reconstituted vial within a limited number of weeks rather than treating it as indefinitely stable. Keep it away from light as well as heat, since peptides also break down under prolonged light exposure, which is part of why vials typically come in amber or opaque glass.

### Handling the vial between uses

Clean the vial top with an alcohol swab before each time it's accessed, and return it to the fridge promptly afterwards rather than leaving it out. This reduces the risk of contamination introduced each time the vial is opened, on top of the temperature and time factors already at play.

## A simple weekly routine

Refrigerate on arrival, note the reconstitution date on the vial itself so the usable window is easy to track, keep it off the door shelf, and check for cloudiness, particles, or an unexpected colour change before each use. If any of those appear, discard the vial regardless of how many weeks have passed since reconstitution. None of this requires special equipment beyond a working fridge and a bit of consistency.
