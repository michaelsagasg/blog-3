## Your fridge's own display isn't reliable enough on its own

Most fridges, including dedicated mini fridges, either lack a temperature display or show a dial setting (like "3" or "max") rather than an actual reading in degrees. A separate thermometer is the only way to confirm your peptides are actually sitting within the 2-8°C range that storage guidance calls for, rather than assuming the dial setting is accurate.

### Why this matters more than it seems

Fridge temperature isn't uniform. The door shelf runs warmer than the back, and a fridge shared with food gets opened often enough that the internal temperature swings more than people expect. A cheap thermometer placed near where you actually store your vials tells you what's happening in that specific spot, not just the fridge's average.

## What to look for when buying one

- **A digital display with a probe**, rather than a simple analogue dial. A probe on a cable lets you place the sensor directly next to your peptide storage spot while reading the display without opening the door.
- **A stated accuracy range**, ideally within 1°C. Most basic digital models meet this, but cheap analogue dial thermometers can be off by several degrees.
- **Min/max memory**, which records the highest and lowest temperature reached since it was last reset. This is useful for catching a brief warm spell you wouldn't otherwise notice, such as the fridge door being left open overnight.

### Where to buy one in the UK

Basic digital fridge thermometers with a probe are widely available from kitchenware retailers, supermarkets, and general online marketplaces, typically for £5-£15. There's little reason to spend much more unless you specifically want a model with a smartphone app or remote alert feature.

### Data-logging thermometers, if you want more detail

A step up from a basic min/max display, a small data-logging thermometer records temperature readings over time rather than just the two extremes, and some models let you view a simple graph via a companion app. This level of detail isn't necessary for most people, but it can be useful if you're troubleshooting an intermittent problem, such as a fridge that seems to run warm only at certain times of day, and want to see exactly when and how often that happens rather than relying on a single spot check.

## How to use it correctly

Place the probe end directly next to where your peptide vials sit, not floating in open fridge space or against the back wall where it may read colder than the rest of the compartment. Check the reading at roughly the same time each day for the first week to get a sense of your fridge's normal range before relying on it long-term.

### Reset the min/max memory periodically

If your thermometer has min/max memory, reset it weekly and check it before resetting again. A max reading that regularly creeps above 8-10°C suggests something worth investigating, whether that's a fridge door not sealing properly, being opened too often, or a fridge that's simply not holding its setting.

## What a bad reading means

A single slightly elevated reading isn't necessarily cause for concern, since brief fluctuations happen with normal door opening. A sustained reading above roughly 10°C for several hours, or a pattern of readings creeping upward over days, is more worth acting on, whether that means adjusting the thermostat, relocating the fridge away from a heat source, or checking the door seal.

### Pairing a thermometer with good habits

A thermometer only helps if you actually check it. Keeping it somewhere visible, rather than tucked behind items, and building a habit of a quick glance whenever you open the fridge removes most of the guesswork around whether your storage is actually within range.

## Why this complements good delivery packaging

A thermometer addresses storage, not transit. Even a perfectly cold-shipped parcel from a supplier with proper insulated packaging still needs consistent 2-8°C storage once it's in your fridge, and a thermometer is the cheapest, most direct way to confirm that's actually happening rather than assuming it.

## Bottom line

A basic digital fridge thermometer with a probe, placed directly next to your peptide storage spot and checked regularly, costs very little and removes one of the biggest blind spots in home peptide storage: not actually knowing what temperature your fridge is holding.
